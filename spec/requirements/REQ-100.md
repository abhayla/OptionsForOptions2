- id: AC-1
  text: >-
    Core proof, before any other AC is built: on the owner's real account, through the product's own Kite door, one
    trigger for a contract the owner holds is created, read back from Zerodha with the same condition, quantity, side
    and product, then deleted and read back as gone. The run records Kite's exact reply to the create call. If Kite
    refuses it for the missing static IP (for example 403 "No IPs configured for this app"), the result is recorded as

- id: AC-2
  text: >-
    Only conditions Zerodha's trigger itself evaluates are offered as broker-held (for example the underlying index
    crossing a level, or a leg's own price crossing a level, as Zerodha's trigger types state at build time). A
    condition Zerodha cannot hold, such as a strategy P&L of minus Rs 5,000, is not offered as broker-held; it is
    offered as a platform-run stop (AC-11), and the screen says which kind each stop is (AC-12). Until the launch
    dependencies of AC-13 are met, a P&L condition on a real account stays an ADR-009 alert with a prepared exit
    basket, and the screen says so in words ("This condition is watched by the app, not held at Zerodha").
- id: AC-3
  text: >-
    The exit orders the trigger will send are built by the same engine as an exit basket: each leg's quantity is the
    strategy's held net per contract from ADR-105 (never the planned quantity), the side reduces that net, the product
    is the product of the Zerodha position it reduces (ADR-080 (1), MIS per ADR-159), and the price follows the tick
    rounding of ADR-080 (2). A strategy with a held net of zero cannot get a trigger. A broker-held trigger holds the
    buy-back of each short leg only: a trigger cannot sequence orders, so a long-leg exit is never placed as a broker
    trigger; after the short triggers fire, the longs are closed by the phase 2 rules of AC-17 at the app's next run, or
    the user is told the protective legs are still open, with one prepared basket. Example - a strategy holding SELL 65
    NIFTY 24500 CE and BUY 65 NIFTY 24700 CE gets a trigger that BUYs 65 of the 24500 CE; once it fires, the app sells
    the 24700 CE under AC-17 phase 2 or shows "Protective legs still open - close them".
- id: AC-4
  text: >-
    Before creating, the user sees one confirmation that states, per leg, the contract, side, quantity in units and
    lots, product and price, the trigger condition, that Zerodha will send these orders automatically when the
    condition is met even if the app is closed, and that a trigger can fill at a worse price than shown in a fast
    market. Wording follows ADR-003 (no "protects you", no "guaranteed"). Nothing is created without that one explicit
    confirmation, recorded with its time and the strategy version.
- id: AC-5
  text: >-
    Every create request is stored in the database (strategy, plan, legs, condition, confirmation id) before it is sent,
    and a strategy has at most one active trigger set; a second create is refused while one is active or while a
    previous create's outcome is unknown.
- id: AC-6
  text: >-
    Zerodha's answer to a create is handled by state. Stated (a trigger id returned) - stored as "Held at Zerodha", read
    back once to confirm the stored legs match. Absent (reply carries no id) - treated as unknown, read back before
    anything else. Not found (read-back shows no matching trigger) - "Not created - nothing is held at Zerodha", the
    user may confirm again. Failed (Kite error with a reason) - "Not created", Kite's reason shown, never retried
    automatically. Empty (Zerodha's trigger list is empty) - same as not found. Ambiguous (two or more triggers match) -
    "Check your triggers on Kite", the strategy shows a mismatch and no further create is allowed until the user
    resolves it.
- id: AC-7
  text: >-
    A timeout or a lost connection on create is never treated as failed and is never retried blindly (ADR-017): the
    outcome is unknown until a read-back of Zerodha's trigger list settles it per AC-6. Example - create times out at
    10:41:05; the read-back at 10:41:20 finds the trigger with the same legs, so it is stored as held, and no second
    trigger exists.
- id: AC-8
  text: >-
    Creating or changing a trigger needs a live Zerodha session for that user. After the daily 06:00 expiry (ADR-053)
    the action is disabled with "Reconnect Zerodha to set a stop at Zerodha"; a trigger already held at Zerodha is not
    affected by the expiry and the strategy says so ("Held at Zerodha - works without your login").
- id: AC-9
  text: >-
    The owner's kill switch (ADR-127) refuses every trigger create and change with the ADR-127 sentence; it never
    refuses a removal, because removing a trigger sends no order. A reconciliation mismatch on the strategy (ADR-018)
    also refuses a create or change.
- id: AC-10
  text: >-
    Money in the confirmation (prices, any rupee figure) is Decimal from the engine; lot size, tick size and freeze
    limit come from the stored exchange data (REQ-054 AC-4). A leg above the freeze limit cannot be part of a trigger
    (ADR-081/ADR-092: no slicing), and the user is told which leg and why.
- id: AC-11
  text: >-
    A stop on the strategy's total P&L (ADR-162) is watched by the platform server, using the engine's live P&L for the
    strategy; when it is reached, the server sends the exit orders itself, without a second user action, because the
    user confirmed that stop in advance. The exit orders are built exactly as AC-3 describes (held net per contract,
    reducing side, position product, tick rounding) and sent in the sequence of AC-17, only while AC-18 says the stop
    is running. Example - "exit at a loss of Rs 5,000" confirmed at entry; at 14:10 the strategy's P&L reaches -5,000
    and the server closes every leg, shorts first then longs, one at a time (AC-17).
- id: AC-12
  text: >-
    The one confirmation of AC-4 covers a platform-run stop in the same way - per leg contract, side, quantity, product
    and price, the P&L condition, and that OFO2 (not Zerodha) will send these orders automatically when the condition
    is met, that it works only while OFO2 and the user's Zerodha login are active, and that orders can fill at a worse
    price than shown. The screen states which kind each stop is: "Held at Zerodha - works even if you are offline" for a
    price-level trigger, "Run by OFO2 - works only while OFO2 and your Zerodha login are active" for a P&L stop. Nothing
    is armed without that explicit confirmation, recorded with its time and the strategy version. Wording follows
    ADR-003.
- id: AC-13
  text: >-
    Launch dependencies, all required before AC-11 runs on a real account: the static-IP route of ADR-154 working;
    Zerodha's written confirmation of the platform's standing for server-sent orders (algo provider registration and
    algo id, F-17, Q258, Q259); and the Q211 legal review. Until every one is met in writing, AC-11 runs only on replay
    and paper, and on a real account a P&L stop falls back to an alert plus a prepared exit basket (ADR-009), labelled
    as such on screen. A test fails if production configuration turns the real-account part on without those three
    recorded.
- id: AC-14
  text: >-
    No blind retry (ADR-017): an exit order the server sends that times out or loses the connection is treated as
    unknown until reconciliation against Zerodha settles it (ADR-018), and is never resent. A P&L stop fires at most
    once per arming; after it fires or is removed it must be armed again with a new confirmation. Example - an exit
    order times out at 14:10:05; the read-back at 14:10:20 shows it filled, so it is recorded as filled and no second
    order is sent.
- id: AC-15
  text: >-
    Every exit order sent by a platform-run stop carries the per-order tag that ties it to its strategy (ADR-105), so
    fills, the timeline and reconciliation attribute it to that strategy and to this stop.
- id: AC-16
  text: >-
    A platform-run stop cannot run after the daily 06:00 Zerodha token expiry (ADR-053), or while the platform is down,
    and the screen says so: from 06:00 until the user logs in again the stop shows "Not running - reconnect Zerodha to
    arm it", and a platform-run stop is never shown as active when it cannot send. The kill switch (ADR-127) and an open
    reconciliation mismatch (ADR-018) refuse arming and stop it sending, with the ADR-127 sentence.
- id: AC-17
  text: >-
    Exit sequence (ADR-162). An automatic P&L exit closes every leg of the strategy in two phases. Phase 1 buys back
    each SHORT leg, one order at a time, and sends the next only after Zerodha confirms the previous one filled. Phase 2
    starts only after every short is confirmed closed, and sells each LONG (protective) leg, one at a time, each
    confirmed. A long is never sold while any short it protects is still open. A leg that is rejected, unknown or timed
    out halts the sequence where it stands: no later leg is sent, the user is alerted (REQ-101 AC-11), nothing is
    retried (ADR-017) and the leg is reconciled (ADR-018). A halt in phase 1 leaves the remaining longs open, so the
    position stays hedged; a halt in phase 2 leaves only longs, so the risk is limited to the premium paid. A strategy
    with only long legs is exited in phase 2 directly. Broker-held triggers follow AC-3 (short buy-backs only). Example -
    a NIFTY iron condor at -5,000: buy back the 23,000 CE short, confirmed; buy back the 22,600 PE short, confirmed; sell
    the 23,200 CE long, confirmed; sell the 22,400 PE long, confirmed; all four legs are closed. If the 22,600 PE
    buy-back is rejected, the sequence stops, both longs stay open, and an alert is sent, with no retry.
- id: AC-18
  text: >-
    Stale feed. A platform-run stop is evaluated on feed-level health (ADR-015, backend/ofo/marketdata/feed_health.py),
    never on one contract's own tick age: while the feed is unhealthy (no frame or heartbeat within the feed's stale
    window, disconnected beyond the reconnect window, or session ended) the stop shows "Not running - prices not
    updating, as of <time>" and does not fire. A quiet contract alone is not stale - on a healthy feed on 2026-10-08 a
    median 59 of 1,091 contracts had no tick for over 60 s (F-32). Before firing, the server confirms every leg's price
    with one fresh quote call and fires only if the P&L recomputed from those quotes still meets the condition. Answer
    states of that call - stated (a fresh price for every leg) fires when the condition still holds, otherwise the stop
    stays armed; absent (a leg missing from the reply), not found (a contract Zerodha does not know), empty (no quotes
    returned), failed (an error or a timeout) and ambiguous (two prices for one leg, or a quote time older than the
    trigger evaluation) each block the fire, alert the user, and wait for the next evaluation; none is retried in a
    tight loop.

# REQ-100: Broker-held stop-loss trigger, creation

Why: a beginner who sets a stop-loss wants the position closed when the market goes against them, even if they are
not watching and even if the app or their daily Zerodha login is down (ADR-153). An alert they may not see does not do
that; a trigger held by Zerodha does.

Example: a NIFTY iron condor holding 65 units per leg. The user sets "exit if NIFTY closes above 24,600", reads the
confirmation listing two broker-held exit orders (the buy-back of each short leg; a trigger cannot sequence the long
exits, AC-3), and confirms once. The platform creates the trigger at Zerodha and reads it back; the strategy shows "Stop
held at Zerodha - NIFTY above 24,600 - 2 exit orders". At 14:10 NIFTY gaps to 24,650 while the user is away; Zerodha
sends the two buy-backs. At its next run the app closes the two longs under AC-17 phase 2, or, when it cannot, says
"Protective legs still open - close them" with one prepared basket.

Held until: trigger-endpoint static-IP proof (AC-1; ADR-153, ADR-154) for the build, and the Q211 legal review of the
confirmation wording (ADR-153) for launch. The platform-run P&L stop (AC-11 to AC-18) is held until the static IP of
ADR-154 works, Zerodha confirms the platform's standing in writing (F-17, Q258, Q259) and Q211 clears it (ADR-162).

Keeping the trigger in step with the strategy, its display, removal and firing are REQ-101.

Decisions: ADR-153 (SPEC CHANGE to ADR-009 for exits only), ADR-154, ADR-162 (a P&L stop is executed automatically by the
platform server, after one confirmation; price-level stops stay held at Zerodha).
