title: 'The API process may also connect to the owner''s configured Notifier address; the watcher heartbeat lives in the feed bus store and is judged at 2 minutes'
decision: >-
  Technical call under the owner's approval of the GC-ICSAFE contract (2026-11-10), for the alert half of REQ-115
  AC-6 and for ADR-285 (1). (1) SPEC CHANGE of the runtime egress gate's rule ("this process connects only to
  loopback, the database and the Kite doors"): one more endpoint is allowed, the single address configured as the
  Notifier (host and port of the setting NOTIFIER_URL, read once at import, https or loopback only). Nothing is
  allowed when the setting is empty. No other door changes; the gate still cannot be switched off. (2) What may be
  sent there: operational alerts for the platform owner only (the background watcher stopped or recovered; the
  market calendar is about to end). Never a user's data, an account figure, a position, a token or a symbol list.
  (3) A Notifier that is not configured, unreachable or refusing never breaks the app: the alert is logged as not
  sent with its reason and the check goes on. (4) The background watcher writes the time of each cycle to the feed
  bus store it already uses. The API process reads it once a minute. No heartbeat for 2 minutes in market hours
  (REQ-115 AC-6; the contract's 180 s is not used) is "stopped". One alert is sent for each CHANGE of state in
  market hours (stopped, running again, cannot be read), so the last message the owner holds is always true; at most
  6 a day, after which changes are logged only. An alert counts as sent only when the Notifier accepts it; until then
  it is tried again each minute while that state holds. Outside market hours and on days the calendar does not open,
  it is silent. A heartbeat that cannot be read, or is dated in the future, is "cannot be read", never healthy.
  (First written as "one alert per day per state"; changed the same day after the Tier A review showed a second
  outage on one day would send nothing and leave "running again" as the owner's last message.)
