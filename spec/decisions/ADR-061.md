# ADR-061: Greeks use the parity forward of each expiry

## The three options
| Option | Result on live data 2026-10-08 09:18 (F-32) | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| A. Parity forward / implied dividend yield per expiry | Call-put IV gap at the same strike: 0.04-0.17 vol points | **chosen** |
| B. Index spot, as REQ-072 AC-3 was written | Gap 1.8-2.8 points on NIFTY, 10.5 on SENSEX's expiry day | rejected: the call and put show visibly different IVs |
| C. Futures price | Futures exist for monthly expiries only, so weekly expiries need A anyway | rejected |

## Why A
- Put-call parity (C - P = e^(-rT)(F - K)) holds for European index options by no-arbitrage, so F is read from the
  market rather than assumed. An index pays dividends, so its forward sits below spot e^(rT): NIFTY 13-Oct
  F e^(-rT) = 22,522.53 against spot 22,545.65.
- It is the standard method for index options (Black-76 on the forward; Hull, ch. 17-18), and it needs no new data.
- The engine stays exact: its own IV reprices the market price with Rs 0.00 error on all four chains.
