# ADR-004: Users and UX levels

## Owner answers
- **Q2 = D** (T1 #5): beginner + intermediate, powerful enough for intermediate traders.
- **Q21 = D** (T1 #43): simple by default, Advanced Details on demand; the user does not need to read Greeks to know
  whether a strategy needs attention.
- **Q31 = A** (T1 #79, after the owner said UX must depend on experience, #77): three levels —
  **Guided** (plain language, education, strong risk visibility, guided decisions, Greeks hidden, prevents obviously
  incomplete/invalid strategies, explicit acknowledgement of important risks), **Standard** (templates, strike
  controls, conditions, payoff, Greeks, margin, charges, adjustments, simulation), **Advanced** (manual builder, leg
  configuration, detailed Greeks/IV/expected move, advanced rules, detailed execution controls).
  The choice is asked as *"How much guidance would you like? Guide me / Standard / Advanced"*, not as a self-declared
  expertise test.
- **Q32 = A** (T1 #81): switch any time (Settings → default experience) plus a per-screen **Show advanced details**.
  Even Advanced cannot: place a standalone order, bypass strategy validation, margin checks, broker restrictions, a
  critical broker rejection, or reconciliation blocks.
- **Q76** (T1 #223): the self-declared market experience may shape presentation, never trading permissions.

## Information density (T1 #42)
Default strategy card: status, P&L, target, risk, underlying vs range, "no action needed"/next thing to watch.
Drill-down tabs: Overview · P&L · Risk · Payoff · Legs · Greeks · Conditions · Orders.
