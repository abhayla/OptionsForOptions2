decision: >-
  Owner decided 25 Nov 2026 (night, chat in the orchestrating cloud session; the question tool is closed after 22:00
  IST), one question at a time. Nothing here is built yet; each point names the finding it settles.
  (1) S-21, the plan's stop after part of the trade closes: the rupee amounts of a plan's stop line (the "% of max
  loss" stop and the "2x the credit received" stop of ADR-247) are worked out ONCE, from the trade as entered, and
  stay fixed. They change only when the user edits the plan. Closing a side or a leg does not move them. Real case
  (25 Nov): the plan read "Stop: loss of 15.4 % of max loss (Rs 400.40)" at 10:32 and "(Rs 756.76)" at 12:55 after the
  call side closed, and "2 x credit" went from Rs 7,800 to Rs 3,172, with no message. Not chosen: re-base to the
  remaining position with a notice; ask each time; re-base silently (today).
  (2) S-27, the plan after one side of a trade exits (real case 25 Nov: the call side of an iron condor closed,
  leaving a bull put spread; the plan still read "Iron condor: system default", every rule line read "Lapsed" and no
  new idea appeared). (a) The plan stays the one set at entry and only the rules that still fit keep running: a rule
  for the closed side reads "Not applicable: the <call or put> side is closed"; the rules for the remaining side keep
  working. (b) When a side closes, the user is told so and asked whether he wants to add new positions on the closed
  side. If yes, the app takes him to add the legs on that side, so the trade is again the shape it was (an iron
  condor). If no, he is told that the strategy will then change from its entry shape to the shape that remains
  (iron condor to bull put spread when the call side closed; bear call spread when the put side closed) and asked to
  confirm. If he confirms, the strategy is shown as the new shape from then on; the plan of (a) continues. If he does
  not confirm, he is asked again to add the legs on the closed side. Until he answers, the trade keeps its entry
  shape name with the notice showing. New legs are paper orders of that strategy like any other (ADR-002); nothing
  is added without the user. Not chosen: offer the new shape's own plan; switch the plan automatically; leave it as
  it is.
  (3) S-16, alerts after they fire (real case 25 Nov: "Loss reaches this % of max loss: 15.5750 % (rule 15.4000 %) -
  Lapsed at 11:05:01 IST, fired 11:02:20 IST"; the plan's loss stop was crossed, the line lapsed in under three
  minutes and the loss then grew). Alerts are of two kinds. (a) ACTIONABLE: the user's plan says he should act (the
  plan's loss stop crossed, its profit target reached, an adjustment rule fired). Such an alert does not close, lapse
  or leave the Active list until the user himself acknowledges it and takes his call: exit the position, or continue
  with the position. It shows "Fired at HH:MM"; if its condition later stops being true it adds "no longer true since
  HH:MM" and still stays. His answer and its time are kept with the alert in History. The alert only informs and
  asks: it never exits by itself (decision support, ADR-003; no auto-exit from plan targets). (b) INFORMATIONAL:
  nothing for the user to do (paper orders placed, settled at expiry, stop saved). Such a message is a short pop-up
  that disappears by itself and is kept only in History. Not chosen: keep a fired alert for the rest of the trading
  day; let it lapse when the condition stops being true (today).
  (4) D-29, the bell number (real case 25 Nov: the bell showed 1 while Alerts > Active listed 4 rows): the bell counts
  the actionable alerts of point (3) that the user has not yet answered, and that number equals the number of rows in
  the Active list. Informational messages never count. Not chosen: count everything unread; leave as it is.
  (5) S-10 (with D-34), rupee levels in a plan: (a) a plan's profit target and loss stop can be typed either in rupees
  or in percent, and the plan shows both figures. (b) A rupee level behaves exactly like every other plan level when
  it is hit: it raises an actionable alert of point (3) and nothing exits by itself. One rule for every plan trigger
  (percent, rupees, adjustment rules). The exits a user arms himself on the Stops panel (index conditional exit, leg
  stop) are a different thing and keep exiting as they do today. Not chosen: percent only; an auto-exit switch on the
  plan (may be raised again after the one exit function is screen-tested).
  (6) S-22, a leg stop that would raise the risk (real case 25 Nov: the stop on the short 26000 CE fired, the long
  26100 CE was left alone, and max loss went from -2,860 to -12,005.51 with no warning): when the user sets a stop on
  one leg, the Stops panel shows, before he saves, what the trade becomes if that stop fires: "If this stop fires, max
  loss becomes Rs X (now Rs Y)", from the one engine (ADR-008). The same line is in the alert when the stop fires. It
  informs only and never blocks the save. Not chosen: ask for confirmation when max loss would rise; no warning.
  (7) S-13, time exit on any day: a plan's time exit can be set for any trading day and time, not only expiry day. At
  that time it raises an actionable alert of point (3), like every other plan level; nothing exits by itself. Not
  chosen: an armed timed exit on the Stops panel that exits the paper trade; both; expiry day only (today).
  (8) S-01, deleting many strategies at once: My Strategies gets a tick box per row, "Select all" and "Delete
  selected". One confirmation names how many will be deleted. Every rule of a single delete applies to each row
  (ADR-292, ADR-293): a strategy that cannot be deleted today (for example one with open positions) is skipped and
  named in the result, as in "12 deleted, 2 kept: they have open positions". Not chosen: tick boxes on drafts and
  completed strategies only; one at a time (today).
  (9) S-17, playbooks and plans, the owner's model in his words (25 Nov night; details still being asked, one question
  at a time, and added here as he answers). A PLAYBOOK is one complete adjustment method for a strategy type, with
  all its conditions (for example 20); it comes from one way of adjusting that type (for example one expert's method
  for an iron condor). One strategy type can have SEVERAL playbooks (three adjustment methods for the iron condor =
  three iron condor playbooks). A PLAN belongs to one strategy: when the user sets up the plan of a strategy he made,
  he picks a playbook, sees all of that playbook's conditions, may change all or some of them, and saves; the plan
  then applies only to that open position. A plan is a subset of its playbook. A plan can be saved with a name and
  used again on other strategies. A strategy has exactly ONE active plan at a time. The user may pick any of the
  playbooks offered for his strategy. Three iron condor positions can therefore each run their own plan, each from a
  different playbook, side by side. SPEC CHANGE: REQ-158 ("one read-only system playbook per strategy type") becomes
  several standard playbooks per strategy type.
  Who makes playbooks (owner, same night): the product team writes the STANDARD playbooks for each strategy type (for
  example three for the iron condor, five for another type) and saves them; they are offered to every user of the app.
  A user can make his OWN playbooks: he selects an existing playbook, edits it and saves it under his own name; it is
  his alone. (This part works today: "Save as new playbook".)
  Two levels only (owner, same night): playbooks (standard or the user's own; reusable) and the plan (the copy that
  runs on one strategy). "Save this plan with a name" makes it one of the user's own playbooks; there is no separate
  list of saved plans.
  What a plan may change (owner, same night): when a plan is set up from a playbook it shows every condition of that
  playbook; for each one the user may change its values or switch it off for this trade. He cannot add a condition
  the playbook does not have, so the plan stays a subset of its playbook.
  Reset (owner, same night): a plan has "Reset to playbook": after one confirmation every condition goes back to the
  playbook's values. The plan marks the conditions the user changed or switched off, so he sees how it differs from
  its playbook.
  Which playbooks are offered (owner, same night): a strategy is offered only the playbooks written for its own type
  (an iron condor: the iron condor playbooks, standard and the user's own), so a playbook's rules always fit the
  trade. This reads his "any playbook" as any playbook of that type.
  (10) OD-1 (owner 25 Nov about 10:20 IST by the question tool; wording confirmed 25 Nov night), real and paper
  trades in ONE Positions list. SPEC CHANGE to ADR-201 (2), which chose a separate Paper tab. Every paper row carries
  a clear "PAPER" tag (not only a small "P"). The top shows two totals, "Real P&L" and "Paper P&L", never added
  together (ADR-201's rule stays). Filter buttons All / Real / Paper, default All. Every exit confirmation names
  "Paper" or "Real (Zerodha order)" in its title. Real rows still come from Zerodha first (ADR-016); real contracts
  are grouped by strategy.
  (11) OD-3 (owner 25 Nov 11:46 and 11:50 IST, in chat), only a strategy's own plan applies. A playbook is only a
  source to copy from when a plan is made; it never applies to an open strategy by itself. A strategy with no plan
  gets no adjustment suggestions and no target or stop alerts, and shows "No plan: nothing is watched for this
  strategy". Its Stops-panel index exits and leg stops are separate and keep working. SPEC CHANGE to REQ-158 (the
  order strategy plan > chosen playbook > system default ends) and REQ-161 ("No plan saved yet: default rules apply"
  ends).
  (12) OD-2 (owner 25 Nov 11:02 IST, in chat), exits and their checks are stand-alone functions: one exit function
  ("exit this strategy: these legs, this reason") that any trigger calls, and each condition its own stand-alone
  check (index level, leg price, P&L amount, P&L percent, time of day) that only answers met or not met. The one exit
  function is Phase 4 of the modular-doors plan; the checks and the one actionable-alert module follow it.
  (13) OD-5 (owner 25 Nov about 12:50 IST by the question tool), a live "Plan watch" box beside the live P&L on the
  builder and on the Positions row: one line per trigger (profit target, loss stop, each adjustment rule, the
  Stops-panel index exits and leg stops) showing fires at / now / distance / state, in rupees and percent, live, from
  the one engine (ADR-008). Shown only when the strategy has a plan; otherwise the line of point (11).
  Not in this record: OD-4 (tests for every link, button and function, asserting the outcome) is recorded in the
  25 Nov screen notes and waits for the owner's word to start.
  Owner's standing instruction with these answers (25 Nov night): every "actionable alert" above is raised through ONE
  shared module, called with the kind of condition that was met and its facts; no trigger writes its own alert code.
  The same one-module rule holds for every capability in the product (the modular-doors principle).
